## How we tested

We used Wispr Flow for everyday writing: emails, Slack and Notion, prompts to AI assistants, and code comments in an editor. We judged it on how often the output was usable without editing, how it handled accents and rambling, and where it broke down. Facts (pricing, languages, platforms) are checked against Wispr Flow's own current sources and logged in our fact ledger.

## Accuracy and speed

On clear speech in a quiet room, transcription is strong and the automatic cleanup is the standout: it is the difference between a raw transcript and text you can send. Accuracy drops with heavy background noise and with niche jargon until you add terms to the [custom dictionary](/docs/guides/custom-dictionary/). It handles 100+ [languages](/docs/review/wispr-flow-languages/) with automatic detection. Wispr Flow says it is about 4x faster than typing; in practice the speed win is real for first drafts and short messages, less so when you need exact formatting or code syntax.

## Pricing

There is a permanent free plan with a weekly word cap and no credit card. Paid Pro removes the cap; Growth adds team and security controls; Enterprise is custom. Prices vary by region and change, so we do not quote figures here. See the [pricing guide](/docs/review/wispr-flow-pricing/), the [free plan details](/docs/review/wispr-flow-free-plan/), [is Pro worth it](/docs/review/wispr-flow-pro/), and how to [pay less honestly](/docs/review/wispr-flow-discount/).

## Privacy

By default Wispr Flow processes speech in the cloud. For most work that is fine, but if you handle sensitive material or need on-device-only processing, confirm the current privacy and compliance details on Wispr Flow's site before relying on it. Teams can look at the Growth plan for HIPAA and admin controls. Worried it might not be trustworthy? It is a real, well-funded company; see [is Wispr Flow legit](/docs/review/is-wispr-flow-legit/).

## Who should use Wispr Flow

- **Good fit:** developers, writers, support agents, students, non-native English speakers, and anyone with wrist strain who writes across many apps.
- **Look elsewhere:** Linux users, people who need strictly offline dictation, and those who only dictate occasionally (the free plan or built-in OS dictation may be enough). See the [alternatives](/docs/compare/).
