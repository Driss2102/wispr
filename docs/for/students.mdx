# Wispr Flow for students

<DisclosureNotice />

**For students, Wispr Flow is most useful for two things: capturing notes and ideas quickly, and getting a rough essay draft out of your head so you can edit it into shape. It handles 100+ languages, which helps if you study in a second language. It does not do your thinking for you, and dictated drafts still need real editing and citation.**

## Where it helps

- **Notes and summaries.** Talk out what you just read or heard; get a tidy note.
- **Essay first drafts.** Speak the argument, then structure and edit it.
- **Studying in another language.** Dictate in your first language or your study language.

## An honest note on integrity

Dictation is just a faster way to write your own words; that is fine. Using an AI to write the essay for you is a different thing and is what your school's rules are about. Wispr Flow transcribes what you say, so use it to get your own ideas down faster, then do the real work of editing and citing.

## A simple study workflow

1. After a lecture or reading, dictate a quick summary into [Notion](/docs/use/notion/) or [Obsidian](/docs/use/obsidian/).
2. For essays, dictate a messy first draft, then edit by keyboard.
3. Keep a [custom dictionary](/docs/guides/custom-dictionary/) of course terms and names.
