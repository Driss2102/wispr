# Who uses Wispr Flow?

<DisclosureNotice />

**Wispr Flow is used most by people who produce a lot of text across many apps and want to do it faster: solopreneurs and founders, developers, writers, customer-support teams, students, and non-native English speakers. The common thread is high writing volume in short bursts, exactly where speaking beats typing. Below is who gets the most from it, and why.**

## Solopreneurs and founders

If you run a business alone or lead a small team, you write constantly: emails, DMs, sales replies, docs, social posts, and AI prompts. Dictating this bulk frees time and hands. Solopreneurs are a core Wispr Flow audience because every minute saved on writing goes straight back into the business. See [Wispr Flow for solopreneurs](/docs/for/solopreneurs/).

## Developers

Developers use it for the natural-language side of coding: prompts to AI assistants in [Cursor and VS Code](/docs/use/cursor/), comments, commit messages, and issues. Not for exact syntax. See [Wispr Flow for developers](/docs/for/developers/) and [how to dictate code](/docs/guides/dictate-code/).

## Writers and content people

Anyone who drafts for a living uses it to beat the blank page: talk out a messy first draft, then edit. See [Wispr Flow for writers](/docs/for/writers/).

## Customer-support and ops teams

High-volume, time-pressured replies are ideal for dictation, paired with canned macros. See [Wispr Flow for customer support](/docs/for/customer-support/).

## Students and non-native speakers

Students use it for notes and essay drafts; non-native English speakers use it to write cleanly in a language they speak more fluently than they type. See [for students](/docs/for/students/) and [for non-native speakers](/docs/for/non-native-english-speakers/).

## People with RSI

A large group comes to dictation to reduce typing strain. See [Wispr Flow for RSI](/docs/for/rsi/).

## Who does not use it much

Occasional writers, people who need offline-only processing, and Linux users, who have no native app. If that is you, read the [alternatives](/docs/compare/) instead.
