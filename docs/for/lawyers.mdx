# Wispr Flow for lawyers

<DisclosureNotice />

**Lawyers write constantly, emails, memos, client notes, and first drafts, and Wispr Flow can speed all of that up by letting you dictate instead of type. The honest caveats matter here more than in most fields: it is general dictation, not a specialized legal-vocabulary tool like Dragon Legal, and it processes speech in the cloud by default, so confidentiality and privilege deserve a careful look before dictating sensitive matter.**

This is not legal or compliance advice. Confirm your firm's rules and Wispr Flow's current terms before using it on client matters.

## Where it helps

- **Correspondence and memos.** Prose you write all day, faster by voice.
- **Notes and summaries** after calls or meetings.
- **First drafts** you then edit precisely by keyboard.
- **Custom dictionary** for recurring party names, case terms, and citations. See the [custom dictionary guide](/docs/guides/custom-dictionary/).

## The honest limits for legal work

- **Not a specialized legal tool.** It does not ship deep legal vocabularies the way Dragon Legal does; you build accuracy through the custom dictionary.
- **Confidentiality.** Cloud processing by default means you should not dictate privileged or sensitive content without checking firm policy and Wispr Flow's [privacy terms](/docs/review/is-wispr-flow-safe/). Teams can look at the Growth plan for admin and HIPAA-grade controls.
- **Exact citations and figures** should be typed and checked, not trusted to dictation.

## A cautious workflow

1. Use it for non-sensitive drafting and internal notes first.
2. Build a dictionary of your recurring terms.
3. Keep privileged material off cloud dictation unless your firm has cleared it.
