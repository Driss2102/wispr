# Wispr Flow for dyslexia

<DisclosureNotice />

**For many people with dyslexia, the hardest part of writing is spelling and the effort of getting words onto the page, not having something to say. Dictation removes much of that friction: you speak, and Wispr Flow produces correctly spelled, punctuated text. It is a genuinely helpful accessibility tool for writing, though it is an aid, not a replacement for whatever support or tools you already rely on.**

This is a practical accessibility take, not medical or educational advice.

## Why it helps

- **No spelling barrier.** You say the word; the app spells it. That removes a major source of friction and fatigue.
- **Punctuation handled.** Commas and periods are added automatically, so you focus on the ideas.
- **Confidence.** Getting clean text out quickly can make writing feel less daunting.

## Honest limits

- **It hears what you say.** If you are unsure of a word, dictation will not choose a better one for you; pair it with an AI assistant you can [dictate to](/docs/use/chatgpt/) for rewriting help.
- **Names and jargon** still need a [custom dictionary](/docs/guides/custom-dictionary/) to come out right.
- **Review still matters.** Read back the text (or have it read aloud) to catch anything that came out wrong.

## A comfortable setup

Dictate in whatever app you already write in, keep a quiet space and a [decent microphone](/docs/guides/best-dictation-microphone/), and add your common words to the dictionary. The goal is to make writing lower-effort, not perfect on the first pass.
