# Wispr Flow for RSI and wrist strain

<DisclosureNotice />

**If typing hurts, dictation is one of the most effective ways to cut keystrokes. Wispr Flow lets you offload emails, messages, notes, docs, and AI prompts to your voice, which can meaningfully reduce the load on your hands. It is a real tool for managing RSI, though it is a complement to proper ergonomics and medical advice, not a cure.**

This is not medical advice. If you have pain, see a professional. What follows is how the tool can help reduce typing.

## How it reduces keystrokes

- **Prose goes to voice.** Email, chat, notes, and prompts are the bulk of most people's typing and all dictate well.
- **You keep the keyboard for precision.** Short edits and exact syntax, so you are not straining for the easy stuff.
- **Hotkey, not mouse.** A [good hotkey](/docs/guides/keyboard-shortcuts/) means you rarely reach awkwardly.

## A gentle setup

1. Start with the highest-volume task that hurts (often email or chat).
2. Use a comfortable [hotkey](/docs/guides/keyboard-shortcuts/) you can hold without strain.
3. Add your common words to a [custom dictionary](/docs/guides/custom-dictionary/) so you correct less (correcting is typing).

## An honest caveat

Dictation trades wrist load for voice load; if you also have vocal strain, pace yourself. And it works best in a space where you can talk. But for keyboard-driven RSI specifically, moving prose to voice is one of the biggest reductions in typing available.
