# The best microphone setup for dictation

<DisclosureNotice />

**For dictation accuracy, the microphone often matters more than the app. A decent mic close to your mouth in a quiet room beats an expensive dictation tool used with a distant laptop microphone in a noisy space. You do not need studio gear: a good headset mic, consistent positioning, and low background noise get you most of the way.**

## What kind of microphone

- **A headset mic** (wired or wireless) close to your mouth is the sweet spot for dictation: consistent distance, good signal, hands-free.
- **A decent USB mic** works well at a desk if you keep a steady distance.
- **Built-in laptop mics** work but are the weakest option: far from your mouth and prone to picking up room noise.
- **AirPods and earbuds** are convenient and fine for casual use, though a dedicated headset mic is usually clearer.

You do not need an expensive microphone. Consistency and closeness matter more than price.

## Positioning and environment

- **Keep the mic close and at a steady distance.** Trailing off or leaning back mid-sentence causes errors.
- **Reduce background noise.** A quiet room is the single biggest accuracy boost.
- **Avoid plosives.** Position the mic slightly off to the side so hard "p" and "b" sounds do not pop.

## Software side

Set the correct input device in both Wispr Flow and your OS sound settings, then add your common terms to a [custom dictionary](/docs/guides/custom-dictionary/). If audio is failing entirely, see [fix microphone problems](/docs/guides/fix-microphone/).

## The honest takeaway

If your dictation accuracy is poor, check the microphone before blaming the app. A better mic and a quieter room usually fix more than any setting. See [Wispr Flow accuracy](/docs/review/wispr-flow-accuracy/) for what to expect once your audio is good.
