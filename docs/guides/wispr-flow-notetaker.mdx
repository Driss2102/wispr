# Wispr Flow Notetaker

<DisclosureNotice />

**Wispr Flow Notetaker is the app's meeting-notes feature: it captures notes from your meetings so you can review, share, and reuse them, including inside AI tools like Claude and ChatGPT. It is a growing part of Wispr Flow (the company is expanding beyond pure dictation into meetings), but it is not yet a full replacement for a dedicated meeting assistant like Otter. What you get depends on your plan.**

## What Notetaker does

Notetaker turns a meeting into usable notes rather than a raw recording. The captured notes can be fed into AI assistants, so you can ask Claude or ChatGPT to summarize, extract action items, or draft a follow-up from them. It sits alongside the core dictation feature, so one app covers both "type by voice" and "capture this meeting".

## What you get by plan

- **Free:** limited Notetaker access (on Mac), with notes usable in Claude and ChatGPT.
- **Pro:** extended Notetaker history.
- **Growth:** unlimited Notetaker access, plus team and security controls.

Exact limits change, so confirm current details on the [pricing page](/docs/review/wispr-flow-pricing/) or Wispr Flow's site.

## Notetaker vs a dedicated meeting tool

If meeting notes are your main need, a purpose-built assistant like [Otter](/docs/compare/wispr-flow-vs-otter/) still goes deeper on speaker separation and polished summaries. Notetaker's advantage is that it comes with the dictation app you may already be using, and its notes flow naturally into AI tools. For many people that is enough; for heavy meeting workflows, a dedicated tool may fit better.

## Who Notetaker suits

- People who already dictate with Wispr Flow and want light meeting capture in the same app.
- Anyone who wants meeting notes they can drop straight into Claude or ChatGPT.
- Less ideal for teams needing deep, standalone meeting-intelligence features.
