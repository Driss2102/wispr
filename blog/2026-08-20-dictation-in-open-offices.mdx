Voice dictation is one of the fastest ways to write, and one of the most awkward to use around other people. If you work in an open office or a shared room, the tool is not the problem; the social contract is. Here is how to get the speed without becoming the person everyone glares at.

{/* truncate */}

## Why it feels awkward

Typing is silent; talking is not. Colleagues can hear your half of every message, which feels like eavesdropping to them and exposure to you. That discomfort, not accuracy, is why most office workers abandon dictation. Understanding that the barrier is social, not technical, is what lets you work around it.

There is also a self-consciousness loop: the first few times you dictate near others, you monitor your own voice, which makes you slower and stiffer, which makes it feel worse. That fades within a few days once you realize nobody is actually listening as closely as you fear.

## Practical etiquette

- **Keep it quiet and short.** Dictate at a low, even volume in brief bursts, not a running monologue. A headset lets you speak softly and still be understood.
- **Pick your moments.** Draft longer pieces when the room is empty, early morning, lunch, or when you step into a booth.
- **Use a headset.** It signals "I am in my own audio bubble" and improves accuracy at lower volume, which lets you speak more quietly. See [the best microphone setup](/docs/guides/best-dictation-microphone/).
- **Step out for sensitive content.** Anything confidential should not be spoken across a shared desk anyway. See [cloud vs on-device dictation](/blog/cloud-vs-on-device-dictation/).
- **Mobile as an escape hatch.** The phone app lets you dictate in a quiet corner and sync the text back, which is perfect for longer replies. See [Wispr Flow on iPhone](/docs/use/iphone/).

## Reading the room

Different offices have different norms. A busy sales floor where everyone is already on calls is a fine place to dictate; nobody notices one more voice. A silent, library-style engineering room is not. Match your behavior to the ambient noise: if the room is quiet enough that a whisper carries, type. If it already has a hum of conversation, voice blends in.

## When not to dictate

Sometimes typing is simply the polite choice: in a silent office, in meetings, or when the content is private. Voice is a tool, not a mandate. Use it where it helps and switch to the keyboard where it does not. The people who keep dictation long-term are the ones who switch fluidly, rather than forcing voice everywhere and irritating those around them.

## The honest trade-off

Dictation trades keystrokes for a bit of social friction. In a private room or at home, the trade is all upside. In a packed open office, weigh it, and lean on the phone, a headset, and good timing to keep the friction low.

## FAQ

**Is it rude to dictate in an open office?**
It can be, if you talk loudly or constantly. Keep it quiet and brief, use a headset, and save longer dictation for quiet moments, and most colleagues will not mind.

**How do I dictate at work without disturbing people?**
Use a headset, speak softly in short bursts, and match the room. In a noisy office voice blends in; in a silent one, type or step away.

**Where should I dictate private or confidential content?**
Not across a shared desk. Step into a private space, and remember cloud dictation sends audio off-device by default.
