Voice dictation is one of the fastest ways to write, and one of the most awkward to use around other people. If you work in an open office or a shared room, the tool is not the problem; the social contract is. Here is how to get the speed without becoming the person everyone glares at.

{/* truncate */}

## Why it feels awkward

Typing is silent; talking is not. Colleagues can hear your half of every message, which feels like eavesdropping to them and exposure to you. That discomfort, not accuracy, is why most office workers abandon dictation.

## Practical etiquette

- **Keep it quiet and short.** Dictate at a low, even volume in brief bursts, not a running monologue.
- **Pick your moments.** Draft longer pieces when the room is empty or you are on a break.
- **Use a headset.** It signals "I am in my own audio bubble" and improves accuracy at lower volume.
- **Step out for sensitive content.** Anything confidential should not be spoken across a shared desk anyway.
- **Mobile as an escape hatch.** The phone app lets you dictate in a quiet corner and sync the text back.

## When not to dictate

Sometimes typing is simply the polite choice: in a silent library-style office, in meetings, or when the content is private. Voice is a tool, not a mandate. Use it where it helps and switch to the keyboard where it does not.

## The honest trade-off

Dictation trades keystrokes for a bit of social friction. In a private room or at home, the trade is all upside. In a packed open office, weigh it.
