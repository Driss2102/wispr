How much faster is talking than typing, really? Here is a sourced roundup of what research and vendors say about dictation speed, accuracy, and adoption. Where a number is a company's own claim, we label it as such, and where it comes from research, we cite it.

{/* truncate */}

## Speed: speaking vs typing

- **Typing:** average typing speed is commonly cited at around 40 words per minute for most people, with skilled typists faster. Treat this as a general benchmark rather than a precise figure, since it varies widely by person and keyboard.
- **Speaking:** conversational speech is typically around 120 to 150 words per minute, far above average typing.
- **Research:** a 2016 study by Ruan and colleagues (Stanford University, University of Washington, and Baidu Research) found that speech input on a smartphone was roughly three times faster than typing on the same device, and with a lower error rate after correction. This remains one of the most cited controlled comparisons of speech versus keyboard input.

Source: Ruan et al., "Speech Is 3x Faster than Typing for English and Mandarin Text Entry on Mobile Devices" (2016).

## Accuracy

Modern speech recognition on clear audio is strong, but published accuracy figures depend heavily on the test conditions (noise, accent, vocabulary). Rather than cite a single headline "accuracy percent" that does not generalize, the honest summary is: accuracy is high on clear speech and common vocabulary, and drops with noise, strong accents, and jargon. That pattern is consistent across tools, including [Wispr Flow](/docs/review/wispr-flow-accuracy/).

## Vendor claims (labeled as claims)

- **Wispr Flow** states its dictation is about 4x faster than typing, citing roughly 220 words per minute versus 45. This is the company's own marketing claim, not an independent measurement, and it is in the same direction as the research above.

## What to take from this

The direction is well supported: for most people and most prose, speaking is meaningfully faster than typing. The exact multiple depends on you, your device, and your environment. The practical implication is to use voice for prose and drafts, where the speed gap is real, and the keyboard for precise edits, where it is not.
