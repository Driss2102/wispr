How much faster is talking than typing, really? Here is a sourced roundup of what research and vendors say about dictation speed, accuracy, and adoption. Every research number below traces to a named, dated study, and every vendor number is labeled as a company claim. If you cite one figure from this page, make it one with a study behind it.

## Speed: speaking vs typing

- **Average typing speed is about 52 words per minute.** The largest typing study to date measured 168,000 volunteers and 136 million keystrokes and found a mean of 51.56 wpm, with individual averages ranging from 4 to 158 wpm (Dhakal et al., CHI 2018).
- **Speech entry was about 3x faster than the keyboard on phones.** In a controlled Stanford-led study, English speech input ran at 153 wpm versus 52 wpm for the keyboard, a 2.93x speed-up (Ruan et al., 2016).
- **The gap held in a second language.** Mandarin speech input was 2.87x faster than the keyboard (123 vs 43 wpm) in the same study (Ruan et al., 2016).
- **Conversational speech is roughly 120 to 150 wpm.** This is a widely used general benchmark rather than a single study, and it varies by speaker and language.

The direction is consistent: for prose, speaking is meaningfully faster than typing. The exact multiple depends on you, your device, and your environment.

## Accuracy

- **Speech had a lower error rate than the keyboard, not just a higher speed.** In the Stanford-led study, the English error rate was 20.4% lower with speech, and the Mandarin error rate 63.4% lower, than typing on the phone keyboard after correction (Ruan et al., 2016).
- **The test used a strong recognizer.** Those results came from Baidu's Deep Speech 2 against the built-in iOS QWERTY and Pinyin keyboards (Ruan et al., 2016), so treat them as speech recognition at its best, not every app on every device.
- **Real-world accuracy depends on conditions.** Published accuracy figures swing widely with noise, accent, and vocabulary. The honest summary: accuracy is high on clear speech and common words, and drops with noise, strong accents, and jargon. ## How to read dictation statistics

- **Separate research from marketing.** A peer-reviewed study with a named method carries more weight than a number on a pricing page. This page labels which is which.
- **Watch the device.** Most speed research is on phones, where thumb typing is slow and speech wins big. On a full keyboard the gap narrows.
- **Watch the task.** Every speed figure here is for prose. None of it applies to code, exact data, or heavy formatting, where typing still wins. See [voice typing vs typing](/blog/voice-typing-vs-typing/).
- **Be wary of "search volume" claims.** Trend indices are relative, not absolute counts, so treat any "X million searches" figure skeptically unless it names a source.

## What to take from this

The evidence for the core claim is solid: for most people and most prose, speaking is faster than typing, and good speech recognition can also be more accurate on mobile. The exact numbers depend on your device, your environment, and what you are writing. The practical implication is to use voice for prose and drafts, where the speed gap is real, and the keyboard for precise edits, where it is not.
