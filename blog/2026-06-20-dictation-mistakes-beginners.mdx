Most people who try dictation and quit do so in the first week, and usually for the same handful of reasons. None of them are the tool being bad; they are habits that are easy to fix. Here are the five most common mistakes, why they happen, and how to get past each one.

{/* truncate */}

## 1. Over-narrating punctuation

Old dictation trained people to say "comma" and "period" out loud. Modern tools add punctuation automatically, so saying it on top can actually make the output worse, littering your text with the literal words "comma" and "full stop."

**Fix:** speak in natural sentences and let the app punctuate. Pause where you would naturally pause, and trust the cleanup. If you need precise formatting like bullet points or line breaks, learn the few commands your tool supports instead of narrating every mark. See [punctuation and formatting](/docs/guides/dictate-punctuation-formatting/).

## 2. Trying to switch everything at once

Going all-in on voice for every task is overwhelming, so people bounce off it after two frustrating days. Dictation is a skill, and skills need a narrow starting point.

**Fix:** pick one recurring task and dictate only that until it is a habit. The best first task is your first email of the day: it is short, it is prose, and you do it anyway. Once that feels natural, add a second task, then a third. Our [how to use Wispr Flow](/docs/guides/how-to-use-wispr-flow/) guide walks through a gentle first week.

## 3. Fighting it on the wrong tasks

Dictating exact code syntax, precise numbers, or heavily formatted spreadsheets is slow and annoying, and it sours people on the whole idea. They conclude "dictation does not work" when really they picked the one job it is worst at.

**Fix:** dictate prose (email, chat, docs, prompts) and type the precise bits. This split is not a compromise; it is how experienced users work. Developers, for example, dictate comments, commit messages, and AI prompts, and type the actual syntax. See [how to dictate code](/docs/guides/dictate-code/) for the full breakdown.

## 4. Ignoring the microphone

People blame the app for accuracy problems that are really a distant laptop mic in a noisy room. Speech recognition is only as good as the audio it receives, and a built-in laptop mic two feet away in an open office is a hard input.

**Fix:** use a closer mic in a quieter space. A basic headset often does more for accuracy than switching apps, because it sits near your mouth and cuts room noise. If words are still coming out wrong, work through [fix microphone](/docs/guides/fix-microphone/) before you give up on the tool. See also [the best microphone setup](/docs/guides/best-dictation-microphone/).

## 5. Never building a dictionary

Correcting the same names and jargon every day is exhausting and feels like the tool is failing you. Your name, your company, your product, and your field's vocabulary are exactly the words a general model gets wrong.

**Fix:** add your most-corrected words to a [custom dictionary](/docs/guides/custom-dictionary/) once, and stop repeating the work. Ten minutes of setup removes the single most annoying part of daily dictation. This is the step most beginners skip and most long-term users swear by.

## A realistic first week

If you want a simple plan: day one, dictate only your first email. Days two and three, add short chat replies. Day four, add your dictionary. By day five, try a longer draft and notice where voice helps and where you reach for the keyboard. By the end of the week you will have a personal map of what belongs to your voice and what belongs to your hands, which is the real goal. For more, see [how to dictate faster](/docs/guides/how-to-dictate-faster/).

## The takeaway

Dictation has a short learning curve, and almost every "it does not work for me" traces back to one of these five habits. Fix them and give it a real week rather than a frustrated afternoon.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to get comfortable with dictation?**
Most people need about a week of daily use on one or two tasks. The learning is mostly unlearning old habits like narrating punctuation.

**Why is my dictation so inaccurate at first?**
Usually the microphone and a missing dictionary, not the app. Move the mic closer, cut room noise, and add your common names and jargon.

**Should I dictate everything?**
No. Dictate prose and drafts; type code, exact numbers, and one-word fixes. The split is what makes it stick.
