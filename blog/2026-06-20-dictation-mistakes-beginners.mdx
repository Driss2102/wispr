Most people who try dictation and quit do so in the first week, and usually for the same handful of reasons. None of them are the tool being bad; they are habits that are easy to fix. Here are the five most common mistakes and how to get past them.

{/* truncate */}

## 1. Over-narrating punctuation

Old dictation trained people to say "comma" and "period". Modern tools add punctuation automatically, and saying it out loud on top can make the output worse. Fix: speak in natural sentences and let the app punctuate. See [punctuation and formatting](/docs/guides/dictate-punctuation-formatting/).

## 2. Trying to switch everything at once

Going all-in on voice for every task is overwhelming, so people bounce off it. Fix: pick one recurring task (your first email of the day) and dictate only that until it is a habit, then add another.

## 3. Fighting it on the wrong tasks

Dictating exact code syntax or precise numbers is slow and annoying, and it sours people on the whole thing. Fix: dictate prose (email, chat, docs, prompts) and type the precise bits. See [how to dictate code](/docs/guides/dictate-code/).

## 4. Ignoring the microphone

Blaming the app for accuracy problems that are really a distant laptop mic in a noisy room. Fix: use a closer mic in a quieter space. See [the best microphone setup](/docs/guides/best-dictation-microphone/).

## 5. Never building a dictionary

Correcting the same names and jargon every day is exhausting and feels like the tool is failing. Fix: add your most-corrected words to a [custom dictionary](/docs/guides/custom-dictionary/) once, and stop repeating the work.

## The takeaway

Dictation has a short learning curve, and almost every "it does not work for me" traces back to one of these five habits. Fix them and give it a real week. If you want a tool to try it with, we review [Wispr Flow](/docs/review/) honestly, including where it is not the right fit.
