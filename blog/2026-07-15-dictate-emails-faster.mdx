Email is the ideal candidate for dictation: it is almost entirely prose, it is high volume, and most replies are short. If you only adopt voice for one task, make it your inbox. Here is a routine that works.

{/* truncate */}

## Why email fits dictation so well

Unlike code or spreadsheets, email is just sentences. Modern dictation adds punctuation, drops filler, and can match a professional tone, so a spoken reply reads like a written one. The result is that you answer at the speed you think, not the speed you type.

## A simple routine

1. **Triage first, dictate second.** Scan the inbox and decide what needs a reply. Do not dictate while deciding.
2. **Dictate the reply in one pass.** Say the whole message; do not stop to fix small things.
3. **Review, then send.** Fix names, numbers, and links by keyboard. Everything else is usually fine.

## Keep it human

Dictated email can actually sound warmer than typed email, because you talk the way you speak to people. Do not over-format. A quick, clear, spoken reply often lands better than a stiff typed one.

## Watch-outs

- Check names and figures before sending; those are where dictation slips.
- In an open office, keep it quiet or step away. See [dictation etiquette](/blog/dictation-in-open-offices/).
- Sensitive content: remember dictation is processed in the cloud by default.
