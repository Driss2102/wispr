Most people speak far faster than they type, so on paper voice dictation should be a big upgrade. In practice the win depends on what you are writing. Here is an honest breakdown of what changes when you switch from a keyboard to your voice.

{/* truncate */}

## Where voice typing wins

- **First drafts.** Getting messy thoughts out loud beats staring at a blank page. You edit afterward.
- **Short messages.** Slack replies, emails, and chat prompts are faster spoken than typed.
- **Sore wrists.** If typing hurts, voice input is a genuine relief, not just a speed play.

## Where it does not

- **Exact formatting and code syntax.** Dictating brackets, indentation, and precise punctuation is slower than typing.
- **Noisy or shared spaces.** Accuracy drops with background noise, and talking out loud is not always socially fine.
- **Very short edits.** Fixing one word is faster with a keyboard than re-dictating.

## How to make it stick

Start with one habit: dictate your first email of the day instead of typing it. Add a [custom dictionary](/docs/guides/custom-dictionary/) for names and jargon so you stop correcting the same words. Within a week you will know which tasks belong to your voice and which belong to your hands.
